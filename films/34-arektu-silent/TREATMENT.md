# আরেকটু থাকো — "Stay a Little Longer" · treatment (1920s Silent Film)

**Song:** «আরেকটু থাকো», Rapar Rubel (Rubel Miah), 4:04. The artist's master is the soundtrack (his choice) in place of the
cinema pianist; the projector purr sits under it. **Format:** 4:3 print in a picture house, 1920×1080, intertitles in Bangla
with an English line, no lyric captions.

## Logline
Dacca, 1927. In a small picture house, the projectionist in the booth and the pianist at the foot of the screen have shared
every evening show without a word. Tonight is her last show: tomorrow she takes the morning train. He has one machine that
controls time — the projector — and he tries everything with it to make the night last a little longer.

## Three candidate structures
1. **The last show (chosen).** One night in the picture house, then the station at dawn, then the empty booth. The projector is
   time: he undercranks to slow the film, holds the crank to freeze it, winds it back. The film runs out anyway. A love story
   told through a machine; native moves: the chain in one take (tea on a string across the hall), the near-miss (his hand where
   hers was a frame ago), the iris on the look, the trick shot (rewound film), and the ending where the print burns through.
2. **Letters on the screen.** He sends her messages by scratching words onto the leader of each reel; she answers with chords.
   Pretty, but needs many cards, and the style says a card must not do the picture's work.
3. **The station melodrama in three tints.** Amber room, blue rain, rose dawn: a classic farewell. Clear, but it is the story
   the earlier films already told; nothing a silent film does better.

## Characters (silhouette marks)
- **He — the projectionist.** Shirt sleeves rolled, waistcoat, a cloth cap pushed back; deadpan; the body acts (hands on the
  crank, shoulders when he gives up).
- **She — the pianist.** Pale sari with a dark border, long plait, a shawl; at the upright piano below the screen.

## Print plan (tint and damage as mood)
Warm amber, light damage for the evening show; colder and harsher at the ticket; rose and clean for the frozen frame; blue for
the station at dawn; the burn-through at the end. One colour at most: none (the song carries the warmth).

## Sound
The song master; under it, quietly: projector lamp click and motor spin-up at the start, the claw purr throughout the booth
shots, the film tail flapping when the reel runs out, the purr stopping at the frozen frame and at the burn. −14 LUFS.

Bangla + English intertitles; 1927 Dacca; no storyboard stop.
